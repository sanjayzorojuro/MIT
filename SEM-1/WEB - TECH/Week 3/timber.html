About Tim Berners-Lee Sir Tim Berners-Lee was born on 8 June 1955 in London, England. He studied physics at the University of Oxford and later worked at CERN, a major scientific research organization in Switzerland. In 1989, Berners-Lee proposed the idea of the World Wide Web as a way for researchers to easily share and connect information. He developed three important technologies that became the foundation of the web: HTML (HyperText Markup Language), HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol), and URLs (Uniform Resource Locators). He also created the first web browser and web server, helping make his vision of a connected information system a reality. In 1994, Berners-Lee founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). The organization works on web standards to keep the web open, accessible, and compatible across different technologies. Berners-Lee has received numerous honors for his contribution to technology, including the 2016 Turing Award. His invention of the web has had a profound impact on education, communication, business, and everyday life. His Vision Berners-Lee has always supported an open and universal web where people can freely access and share information. His work continues to influence the development of the Internet and modern web technologies.
